## November 2, 2026
Super exicted to see this study **[A North American mycorrhizal fungus displays zinc tolerance intraspecific variation and protects pines from zinc toxicity](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2026.09.050")** out in *Current Biology*! In this study, we combined whole-genome resequencing, in vitro Zn tolerance assays, fungal–pine symbiosis bioassays, transcriptomics, and metabolomics to investigate Zn tolerance in the ectomycorrhizal fungus *Suillus tomentosus* and its host, *Pinus contorta*, two widespread symbiotic partners in western North American forests. We found that Zn tolerance was not associated with distinct population genetic structure in *S. tomentosus*. Instead, Zn tolerance emerged as a highly polygenic and largely constitutive trait, associated with Zn exclusion and antioxidant defense mechanisms that helped mitigate metal toxicity. We also demonstrated that *S. tomentosus* protected *P. contorta* seedlings from Zn toxicity by improving their performance under Zn stress, with Zn-tolerant fungal isolates providing modestly greater benefits than Zn-sensitive isolates. Together, our findings highlight the genetic and physiological complexity of fungal metal tolerance and the ecological significance of intraspecific variation in mycorrhizal symbioses. 
  
## October 12, 2026
Excited to share our multi-institutional collaborative study, **["Tree mixing mitigates mulching-induced trade-offs in diversity and interactions of soil decomposers and ectomycorrhizal fungi in frequently burned conifer woodlands"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apsoil.2026.107530)**, published in *Applied Soil Ecology*. In this field study, we investigated how mulching and forest composition shape belowground microbial communities and their ecological interactions in frequently burned conifer woodlands. We found that mulching significantly reduced the alpha diversity of soil decomposers across forest types, while microbial community composition was more strongly influenced by forest type than by management practices. Mulching also consistently decreased the relative abundance and phylogenetic diversity of ectomycorrhizal fungi (EMF) and weakened their associations with free-living microorganisms. Interestingly, mixed-conifer forests supported more abundant and diverse EMF communities and more complex associations between EMF and free-living microorganisms than forests dominated by a single tree species, regardless of mulching treatment. These findings suggest that tree species mixing may help buffer the negative impacts of mulching on EMF communities and their microbial associations. Overall, our study highlights the context-dependent trade-offs of mulching as a fuel-reduction practice and underscores the importance of overstory tree composition in shaping belowground microbial responses to forest management. These findings provide valuable insights into integrating forest biodiversity conservation with fuel-reduction strategies to promote the ecological resilience of fire-prone conifer woodlands.
  
## August 25, 2026
Excited to share our collaborative study **["Integrated straw return enhances soil multifunctionality without exacerbating greenhouse gas emissions in a rice-faba bean rotation system"](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00374-026-02046-z)**, recently published in *Biology and Fertility of Soils*! Our field study demonstrated that integrating rice and faba bean straw return into a rice-faba bean rotation significantly improved soil quality, increasing soil carbon pools, microbial biomass, and enzyme activities by 5.5% to over 140%, without increasing greenhouse gas emissions, compared with no straw return. We also found that this management practice enhanced microbial potential for glycoprotein degradation and extracellular polysaccharide synthesis, strengthened cross-domain microbial interactions, and reduced potential pathogen risks. Together, these findings highlight the synergistic benefits of integrated rice and faba bean straw return for soil health and microbial functioning, offering a promising climate-smart management strategy for subtropical paddy agroecosystems.
  
## August 25, 2026
Thrilled to see another paper **["Biogeographic and climatic determinants of host-specific phyllosphere fungus-secondary metabolite associations"](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-026-03842-z)** published in *Communications Earth & Environment*. In this study, we surveyed 13 cattle ranches across Florida, profiling leaf-associated fungal communities and targeted secondary metabolites in four perennial warm-season grasses. Fungal assemblages and metabolite composition were significantly structured by geography, climate, and host grass species. Warmer, high-irradiance southern sites harbored greater fungal diversity (particularly among foliar endophytes, mycoparasites, and epiphytes) and metabolite chemodiversity, alongside more complex fungal co-occurrence and fungus-metabolite associations. Host identity further partitioned taxa and metabolites across the landscape, yielding site-dependent association patterns that predicted metabolite composition. 
  
## August 20, 2026
Excited to see this paper **["Ectomycorrhizal fungi rewire plant-fungal molecular networks to enhance plant resilience under heavy metal stress"](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41522-026-01091-8)** piblished in *npj Biofilms and Microbiomes*. We found that ectomycorrhizal fungi can restore root-associated fungal communities and reshape fungi-plant interactions toward more targeted and specialized symbiotic partnerships to primarily reactivate pathways involved in metal exclusion and detoxification, core metabolic activities, and nutrient exchangeunder heavy metal stress. This study provides mechanistic insights into how ectomycorrhizal fungi improve plant fitness by reshaping plant-fungal coordination under heavy metal stress, advancing our understanding of mutualistic strategies that support forest ecosystem sustainability in contaminated environments.
  
## April 25, 2026 
Very happy to see this paper **["Host genotype-enriched rhizosphere microbes enhance pea (*Pisum sativum* L.) yield"](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11104-026-08597-2)** published in *Plant and Soil*. We isolated two representative bacterial strains, *Agrobacterium salinitolerans* S1 and *Neobacillus drentensis* S2, from a high-yield pea genotype and found that both exhibited multiple plant growth–promoting traits in vitro. When reintroduced into the rhizosphere of a low-yield genotype, both strains significantly enhanced plant growth and grain yield under greenhouse conditions, with co-inoculation producing stronger effects than single-strain treatments.
